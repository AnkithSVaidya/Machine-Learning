Cleaning Decisions Log

Data Preparation & Feature Selection:
- Chose to use 10 predictors (PhoneService, MultipleLines, OnlineSecurity, OnlineBackup, DeviceProtection, TechSupport,
StreamingTV, StreamingMovies, InternetService_Fiber optic, InternetService_No) and removed the others (PaperlessBilling, Churn,
TotalCharges, gender, SeniorCitizen, Partner, Dependents, tenure, Contract_One year, Contract_Two year, and payment methods).
The 10 selected services directly represent the add-on offerings mentioned in the business question, while demographic and
billing fields don't directly impact monthly charge prediction for service pricing.

- Ensured that all 10 predictors were binary (0/1) indicators with no NaN values. Verified this by checking isin([0,1]).all()
for each column, confirming data integrity.

- Kept the 'target' column as the dependent variable (monthly charges in USD). The data was already properly encoded with
one-hot encoding for categorical features (InternetService, Contract type), which was appropriate for the modeling task.

Model & Split Decisions:
- Used an 80/20 train/test split with random_state=42 to ensure reproducibility and allow others to verify results.

- Chose linear regression over other methods because the assignment specifically requires a multivariable linear regression,
and the high R² (0.999) indicates the linear assumption is valid for this problem.

- Tested a baseline single-variable model using service count (sum of 10 services per customer) to demonstrate that knowing
which services matters far more than just how many. The baseline performed poorly (R² 0.701, MAE $14.24) vs the full model
(R² 0.999, MAE $0.79), validating the multivariable approach.

Data Quality Observations:
- No missing values detected in any of the 10 service columns or the target variable.
- Target variable ranges from $18.25 to $118.75, representing realistic monthly charges.
- Service adoption varies significantly across the dataset, providing good coverage for model training.
